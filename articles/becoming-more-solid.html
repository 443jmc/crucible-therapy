How to Become a More Solid Person

What Does It Mean to Be Solid?

Being solid, in this work, means you do not collapse when the person closest to you disagrees with you. You can stay yourself in a fight, in a sexual freeze, or in a long silence, and stay close without disappearing.

Solid is not cold, and it is not stubborn. A solid person can change their mind because they see something new, not because they cannot stand the look on their partner's face.

Know What You Stand For

You cannot hold onto yourself if you do not know who you are. A lot of people have never written down what they actually believe. They have positions they inherited from family, church, or the last therapist.

What do you want to be like in a hard conversation? What will you not do to keep the peace? Those answers are more useful than a values poster.

Borrowed beliefs are fine if you have actually chosen them. Unexamined ones fold the first time someone you love pushes back. Solidity still allows change, as long as the change is yours and not a collapse.

Practice Self-Soothing

If you need your partner to calm you down, they own your nervous system. Self-soothing is not pretending you are fine; it is feeling the hit and still being able to think.

When you get reactive, stop talking and breathe, and do not make a decision until you can think. That is less glamorous than a toolkit, and it is the part that matters.

What you say to yourself in those minutes counts. "This is unbearable" makes you dumber; "I do not have to answer this second" gives you a chance to stay a person.

Tolerate Discomfort for Growth

If you always pick comfort, you stay at your current level. Growth is usually the conversation you do not want, the position you do not want to hold, the truth you do not want to say.

You do not have to like the discomfort; you have to last through it when it is in service of something that matters. Each time you stay instead of fleeing, attacking, or going blank, you prove you can handle more than last time.

Hold Your Position Under Pressure

The test is whether you can keep your view when someone you love wants you to drop it. That is not the same as refusing to listen. It is staying connected to what you think while you listen.

A lot of people say yes when they mean no. They agree to keep the evening quiet, and the bill comes later in resentment and a thinner self.

You can say, "I hear that you wanted me to agree. I still think what I said." Start small: pick the restaurant, or say the disagreement in a low-stakes conversation. The bigger moments use the same muscle.

Stop Using Your Partner as a Mirror

If you need them to agree, admire, or reassure you in order to feel like a person, they have too much power. Watch for the tell: you share an opinion and wait to see if it lands, tell a story and wait to be impressed, or ask if you still look good.

Self-validation is knowing what you think without a vote. You can still take feedback; you just do not fall apart when the feedback is not the one you wanted.

This is the slow part of the work. Most of us learned to borrow a self in childhood. The freedom on the other side is that you can be known without managing the audience.

Do What You Say You Believe

Integrity here means your conduct matches your values when it costs you. White lies to avoid awkwardness, yeses you do not mean, commitments you do not keep: those chip the self.

Honesty your partner does not want to hear is uncomfortable, and so is saying no. Those short-term hits are how solidity gets built.

Use the Marriage

Schnarch called marriage a people-growing machine because it keeps handing you situations you cannot fake your way through. Every fight is a chance to stay present, and every disappointment is a chance to soothe yourself instead of making them fix your mood.

That does not mean you accept mistreatment. It means you stop treating ordinary pressure as proof the marriage is broken, and treat it as the forge instead.

You Will Lose It Again

Solidity is slow, and you will lose it in the next hard conversation. What changes is that you recover faster than you used to.
